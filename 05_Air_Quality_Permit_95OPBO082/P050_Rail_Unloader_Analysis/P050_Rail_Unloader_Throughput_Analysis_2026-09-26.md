# CEMEX Lyons P050 (cement rail car unloader): how much imported cement was actually unloaded?

*Research compilation prepared 2026-09-26 (MT) from public CDPHE Air Pollution Control Division records. This is analysis, not a primary document. Quotations are from the cited CDPHE records; inferences are labeled **[Inference]**.*

## Short answer
- **The "2025 actual = 50,000 tpy" entry on the APEN received 4/27/2026 matches the permit limit exactly and is not supported by any metered throughput record in the public file.** Every other public record points to about zero rail-unloaded cement from late 2007 until May 2026. **[Inference]** The entry was probably the permit limit entered in the "actual" box.
  - CDPHE inspectors recorded **0 tons** for the rolling 12 months in 2012, 2018, 2020, 2021, 2022 and 2023. Each report states that no (cement) railcar unloading "has occurred since 2007."
  - CEMEX's own APENs reported **0 tons** actual for data years 2016 and 2022.
  - The only documented startup after 2007 is **5/22/2026**. The 2026 first-half semiannual report describes that day as "system startup": air was used to clear water and blockages from the piping, and a procedure was written and employees trained to operate the equipment.
  - The 2026 APEN is internally inconsistent. It lists "actual" = "requested" = 50,000 tpy at 100 tph. It lists uncontrolled PM equal to controlled PM (.55 = .55) although baghouse control of 98–99% is claimed in the permit. The .55 / .36 tpy figures equal 50,000 tons multiplied by the permit's controlled emission factors; the requested emissions (.6 / .4) are the permit caps.

- p.3: "From what year is the actual annual amount? 2025." "Imported Portland Cement | Design Process Rate 100 tph | Actual Annual Amount **50,000 tpy** | Requested Annual Permit Limit **50,000 tpy**." The Finished Product row repeats it.
- p.5: actual annual emissions data year 2025. TSP: uncontrolled **.55**, controlled **.55**; requested uncontrolled .55, controlled **.6**. PM10: .36 / .36; requested .36 / **.4**. Emission-factor source: "See Attached / Permit Cond. 23.2."
- The form contains no note that the actual equals the permitted value or was estimated.
- Arithmetic check. The controlled Condition 23.2 factors sum to 0.60×2% + 0.45×1% + 0.27×2% = 0.0219 lb PM/ton and 0.40×2% + 0.29×1% + 0.17×2% = 0.0143 lb PM10/ton (control efficiencies 98% / 99% / 98%, as listed in the 2023 inspection report p.223). Times 50,000 t, that gives 0.5475 t PM and 0.3575 t PM10, which round to .55 and .36. The emissions are therefore computed from the 50,000-ton cap. The uncontrolled column cannot be right as entered: at 1.32 lb/t uncontrolled, 50,000 t would be about 33 tpy PM.
- Page extracts: `APEN_2026-04-27_p2/p3/p5_...png`.

- **7/20/2026 report for 1/1–6/30/2026 (OnBase 66366349), p.5**, is the first P050 deviation reported in the records reviewed:
  - "Did the deviation occur during: **Startup**. OPERATING PERMIT UNIT IDENTIFICATION: P050 AIRs pt 050 – BH 825-8. Operating Permit Condition Number Citation: Condition 23.4; Condition 19."
  - "Explanation of Period of Deviation: **During system startup a plugged pipe caused dust to discharge from the fan, screw transport, and related equipment.** Duration (start/stop date & time): **5/22/2026 10:59 am – 11:34 am**."
  - "Action Taken to Correct the Problem: Activities were stopped, and **air was used to clear water and blockages from the piping into the silo**. The baghouse was inspected prior to operation."
  - "Measures Taken to Prevent a Recurrence of the Problem: **A procedure was written by a Logistics Manager familiar with the operation of a rail unloading system. Employees were trained and provided a procedure for operating the equipment.**" Deviation Code: 9 – Other.
  - **[Inference]** Water standing in the transfer piping, and the need to write an operating procedure and train staff after startup, fit a system that had been idle for years. They do not fit a system that moved 50,000 tons (about 500 railcars, or roughly 10 cars/week at 48 weeks/year) in 2025.

## 3. P050 history
- 2005–06: CEMEX applied under 05BO0703. Initial Approval permit issued 4/11/2006. First railcars were released from Louisville, KY on 6/21/2006, arrived 7/16/2006, and were emptied 7/28/2006 (the startup date). CDPHE received the self-certification with the startup notice on 7/28/2006. The O&M plan is dated 8/1/2006. The baghouse was renamed BH 725-4 → BH 825-8 by the 8/2006 APEN.
- 7/2006 – mid-2007: about 14,450 tons unloaded in total (2007 inspection).
- Late 2007 – May 2026: no unloading, per CDPHE inspections 2012–2023 and CEMEX APENs for 2016 and 2022 (0 t; 0 h for 2022). There is no public inspection for 2024–25.
- 4/24–27/2026: APEN update stating 2025 actual = 50,000 t, requesting 50,000 t, with 24/7/48 operation.
- 5/22/2026: "system startup." Plugged, water-fouled piping caused a dust-discharge deviation. A new operating procedure was written and staff were trained.
- **[Inference]** The 5/22/2026 deviation is the first reported P050 activity in about 19 years. It is consistent with a restart in May 2026 and inconsistent with 50,000 tons unloaded in calendar 2025.

## 4. Assessment
- The 50,000 tons for 2025 is not corroborated by any measured record. The support for treating it as the permit limit rather than throughput:
  1. It equals both the permit cap and the requested value exactly.
  2. The emissions are back-calculated from 50,000 t, and uncontrolled = controlled.
  3. "Operation began 1969" is boilerplate that does not fit P050 (the same entry appears on the 2023 APEN).
  4. Every independent throughput record since 2012 is 0 t, and CEMEX's own APEN for 2022 says 0 t / 0 h.
  5. The 2025 semiannual reports show no P050 deviation or activity.
  6. The 2026 report documents a "system startup" on 5/22/2026 with water in the lines and a newly written procedure.
- **Not knowable from public records:** the metered monthly and rolling-12 tonnages for 2025 and 2026. CEMEX keeps these under permit condition 23.1 but they are not in the public file. The next CDPHE inspection report, or CEMEX's records on request, would settle it.
