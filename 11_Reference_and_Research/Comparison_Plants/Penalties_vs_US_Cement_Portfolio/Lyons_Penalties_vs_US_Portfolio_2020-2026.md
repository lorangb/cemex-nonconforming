# CEMEX Lyons (CO) fines & penalties vs. the rest of CEMEX's US cement portfolio, 2020 – Sep 2026

Prepared Sat Sep 26, 2026 (MT) from public records. All times Mountain. Companion data: `Lyons_Penalties_vs_US_Portfolio_2020-2026.csv` (same folder). Every revenue figure below is an ESTIMATE (CEMEX does not report plant revenue); the method is in §4. Penalty dollar figures are from public records (sources in §8).

## 1. Headline

- **Lyons: ~$3.40M in cash penalties, Jan 2020 – Sep 2026** ($556k MSHA operator assessments + $2.85M CDPHE air-division penalties), plus a **$616k Supplemental Environmental Project (SEP)** that was part of a 2025 settlement. EPA, water, hazardous-waste, DRMS and Boulder County: $0 found in the window.
- **Estimated share of Lyons revenue: ~0.8%** (range 0.58–0.84%; ~0.95% if the SEP is counted). In the worst single years (2024 and 2026 year-to-date) it was ~2% of estimated plant revenue.
- **The other seven CEMEX US cement plants: 0.02–0.12% of estimated revenue** (median 0.044%). Lyons is ~18× the peer median and ~7× the next-highest plant.
- **Lyons is an outlier on every normalized measure**, and the reason is **Colorado air enforcement, not mine safety**. On MSHA alone Lyons sits mid-pack in dollars (4th of 8) and high but not top per hour worked (2nd of 8, behind Brooksville South FL).
- **In absolute terms these are small amounts everywhere.** Lyons' penalties equal ~0.010% of CEMEX USA revenue over the same period, and ~0.003% of CEMEX consolidated revenue. CEMEX **does not publish a corporate fines total** (not in the 2025 20-F, not in the 2025 Sustainability-related Disclosures), so no like-for-like corporate comparison exists.

## 4. Normalization method (ESTIMATES)

- **Tonnage:** CEMEX 2025 20-F plant "used capacity", meaning 2025 production including grinding. This is held constant for 2020–2026 as an estimate.

  | Plant | 2025 used capacity (t) |
  |---|---|
  | Victorville | 2,625,788 |
  | Balcones | 1,602,625 |
  | Brooksville South | 1,114,963 |
  | Miami | 862,261 |
  | Demopolis | 690,500 |
  | Knoxville | 537,186 |
  | Clinchfield | 470,964 |
  | Lyons | 342,134 |
  | **US total** | **8,246,421** |
  | Ponce (supplementary) | 309,069 |

  Per-plant installed (nameplate) capacity is not in the 20-F. For Lyons, the GEM tracker gives ~0.40 Mt cement / ~0.55 Mt clinker, and EPA's 2013 release gives ~550k tons/yr of kiln capacity. EPA GHGRP clinker data was not used.
- **Price:** USGS average mill value, US, $/metric t:
  - 2020 $125, 2021 $127, 2022 $139, 2023 $152
  - 2024 and 2025 $160 (estimated, USGS MCS 2026)
  - 2026 assumed $160, 3/4 of a year
  - Sum over 6.75 years: 983 $/t-years
  - District ratios use the MYB 2022 Table 11 US average ($138 for 2022, $126 for 2021) as denominator.
- **Regional adjustment:** USGS Minerals Yearbook 2022, Table 11, district average value ÷ US average. Central case uses 2022; the 2021 ratios give an alternative.

  | District | 2022 ratio (central) | 2021 ratio (alternative) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Colorado–Wyoming ($174.05/t) | 1.261 | 1.202 |
  | California | 0.942 | 0.937 |
  | Texas south | 1.047 | 1.006 |
  | Florida | 0.891 | 0.909 |
  | Alabama–Kentucky–Tennessee | 1.001 | 1.028 |
  | Georgia–Maryland–Virginia–West Virginia | 0.960 | 0.937 |
  | Puerto Rico | withheld; 1.0 used | — |

- **Revenue range:**
  - Low = the lower of the two district ratios.
  - High = central × 1.383, which is CEMEX's 2025 US cement product revenue ($1,825M) ÷ 8.246 Mt ÷ $160/t. It overstates mill revenue because it includes imported cement, freight and intersegment sales.
  - **Sanity check:** summed central revenue for the 8 plants (~$7.9B over 6.75 years) is ~63% of CEMEX's reported US cement revenue for the same period (~$12.4B). That is plausible given imports and freight.
- **Hours:** MSHA operator employee hours, 2020 through 2026 Q2. Contractor hours are not available per mine.
- **"Cash penalties"** = MSHA operator current assessed + state/local environmental + federal penalties. SEPs are shown separately.
- **Per ton** = cash ÷ (2025 used capacity × 6.75 years).
- **Per 200k hours** = cash ÷ operator hours × 200,000.

## 7. Summary of results

- **On these estimates Lyons is an outlier on every normalized measure:**
  - Dollars: ~5× the peer median
  - Share of estimated revenue: ~18×
  - Per ton: ~22×
  - Per 200k hours: ~10×
- **The driver is CDPHE air enforcement**: seven settlements totalling $2.85M cash plus a $616k SEP, rising sharply since 2023. No other CEMEX US cement plant had more than $250k in environmental penalties in the window.
- **On mine safety (MSHA) Lyons is unremarkable to moderately high:** mid-pack in dollars, and 2nd-highest per hour behind Brooksville South.
- **Penalties are small against revenue everywhere.** Even at Lyons they are under 1% of estimated revenue over the period, peaking around 2% in 2024 and 2026 year-to-date. At peers they are about 0.02–0.12%. At the corporate level they are around one-hundredth of a percent.
- **The meaningful signal is relative:** Lyons carries nearly half of all penalties across CEMEX's eight US cement plants while making ~4% of the cement.

## 9. Unverified / unavailable / caveats
- **Revenue is estimated.** Production is held at 2025 levels for all years, and regional price ratios come from 2022 (or 2021). Per-plant installed capacity for peers is not published by CEMEX, and GHGRP clinker data was not used.
- **Peer environmental penalties come from ECHO/ICIS.** State non-air penalties (for example FDEP water or waste, TCEQ, GA EPD) are only captured where states report to EPA, so some may be missing. Lyons' CDPHE penalties were verified in the original documents; peer penalties were not, except Demopolis's $210k, which Violation Tracker corroborates.
- **Victorville's 11 × $10,000 MDAQMD entries** (6/23/2023) may be a single settlement split across notices of violation. The total is unaffected.
- **Date discrepancy:** ICIS dates the Lyons 2023-177/178 order 2/21/2024, but the signed COC is dated 4/23/2024.
- **2026 figures are partial.** MSHA 2026 bills ($62,123 at Lyons) are unpaid and still contestable until about 10/10/2026. The 5/11/2026 CDPHE penalty ($855k) is counted as assessed; payment was not verified.
- **Hours:** MSHA operator hours only, through 2026 Q2, while penalties run through September. Contractor hours and violations are excluded from the per-hour cash figures, though the "MSHA proposed (all)" column includes contractors.
- **SEP:** the $616k is a settlement obligation spent in Lyons, not a fine paid to the state. It is reported separately. As of 7/1/2025 only $63k had been spent.
- **No CEMEX corporate fines total exists** in the documents reviewed. The Integrated Report's GRI 2-27 table could not be retrieved.
- **Earlier CDPHE actions** against Lyons (before 2020) and the 2013 federal consent decree fall outside the window and are excluded.
- **Per-plant data file:** `Lyons_Penalties_vs_US_Portfolio_2020-2026.csv` (same folder). In that file, `share_cash_range_lo_pct` = cash ÷ high revenue and `share_cash_range_hi_pct` = cash ÷ low revenue.
- **Verification status at filing.** Re-checked against source documents when this note was added to the repository: the MSHA figures for Lyons (recomputed from the MSHA bulk data now in `08_Operational_History_and_Traffic/MSHA_Federal_Mine_Safety_Record/`), the CDPHE 5/11/2026 consent order ($855,000) and the CDPHE 5/14/2025 consent order ($154,000 cash + $616,000 SEP). The earlier CDPHE amounts ($5,250; $42,000; $40,250; $357,175; $1,392,000), all peer-plant penalties and the CEMEX corporate revenue figures were carried forward from the original research and were **not** re-verified for this filing.
