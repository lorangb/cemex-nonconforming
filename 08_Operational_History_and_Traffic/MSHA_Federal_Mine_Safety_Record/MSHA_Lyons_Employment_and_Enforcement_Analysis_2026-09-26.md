# CEMEX Lyons cement plant: federal MSHA record (Mine ID 0500344)

*Research compilation, 2026-09-26 (MT). Analysis of public MSHA Open Government Data; not itself a primary document. Inferences are labeled **[Inference]**.*

**Source:** MSHA Open Government Data files (https://arlweb.msha.gov/OpenGovernmentData/OGIMSHA.asp), downloaded 2026-09-26 08:54 MT. The server reported them last modified Fri 2026-09-25 05:24 MT. The data files in this folder are extracts for Mine ID 0500344 (see "Files" at the end).

## Short answer
- **Reported staffing had not fallen as of the latest data, which ends June 30, 2026.** In 2026 Q2 CEMEX reported an average of **113 employees** (104 plant/mill, 3 quarry, 6 office) working **60,897 hours**.
  - That is close to the 11-year high of 119 in 2025 Q3.
  - It is well above the 2016–2020 level of about 93–99.
  - The plant/mill crew grew from about 77–82 (2016–2020) to 104–107 (2025 Q3–2026 Q2).
  - 2025 had the most hours worked of any year in the data, which goes back to 2000: 252,309 hours.
- **Any workforce reduction announced after June 2026 cannot appear in this data yet.** Layoff notices dated Sep 22, 2026 are reported in other research notes (see `13_2026_Plant_Status_and_Layoffs/`; not independently verified). Reporting schedule:
  - Q3 2026 (Jul–Sep) is due from CEMEX by Oct 15, 2026 and covers a period before any such separations.
  - Q4 2026 is due Jan 15, 2027. Separations reported to begin Nov 21 would affect about 6 of the quarter's 13 weeks, and the figure is a quarterly average.
  - Q1 2027 is due Apr 15, 2027, and would be the first full-quarter figure.
  - The record therefore gives a baseline (about 113 people, about 104 of them in the plant) but nothing about the size of any reduction.
- **Thin-crew check:** a kiln that keeps running on fewer people would be expected to show fewer employees and more hours per employee (overtime). Hours per employee have run about 500–585 per quarter (roughly 38–45 hours a week) since mid-2022, with no spike. The operator's Mines-file profile says 7 days a week, 12-hour shifts, 2 production shifts plus 1 maintenance shift per day, and "89 employees." The date of that self-reported profile is unknown and it disagrees with the quarterly reports.
- **Some reductions came earlier:**
  - The in-house quarry crew dropped from 7–13 people to a flat **3 people / about 1,560 hours a quarter from 2024 Q2 onward**. That is 3 × 40 h × 13 weeks, so it looks like a standing placeholder or skeleton crew **[Inference]**. Quarry work may have moved to contractors or been scaled back; the data cannot say which.
  - Office staff went from 9 to 6 in 2026 Q1.
- **Safety record:**
  - About 4–8 MSHA inspections a year. From 2020 through Jul 2026 there were 396 citations/orders, 58 of them "significant and substantial" (S&S), with **$573,757 in proposed penalties** (all violators, including contractors; CEMEX as operator: $560,444 proposed, $499,698 paid). None were contested.
  - **The most recent regular inspection (Jul 15–Aug 4, 2026) had a high severity profile:**
    - 31 citations, 12 of them S&S (39%, against 7–24% in 2020–2025).
    - 7 rated "high negligence."
    - The plant's **first 104(d)(1) "unwarrantable failure" citation since 2012**: 30 CFR 56.14100(b), safety defects on equipment not corrected in a timely manner. Rated "reasonably likely" / "fatal" / high negligence, proposed $12,403.
    - Also cited: equipment used beyond its design capacity (56.14205, $11,450, high negligence), scaffold/working-platform problems (56.11027), unsafe access (56.11001, four citations), missed or inadequate shift workplace examinations (56.18002(a)), and repeat housekeeping citations (56.20003(a)).
    - These penalties were billed Sep 10, 2026 (status "Received"). The usual 30-day contest window runs to about Oct 10, 2026. Payment or contest is not yet recorded.
  - **Kiln-related events in the injury/accident file:**
    - **Feb 13, 2025:** kiln burner flashback while lighting the kiln. Two workers were exposed; one received first/second-degree burns and lost 5 days and had 32 restricted days. MSHA issued a 103(k) order and an S&S citation (56.14100(c), defective equipment not taken out of service; likelihood "Occurred"; $15,768).
    - **Oct 3, 2025:** coal-mill line fire. The Hygiene Fire Dept. was called.
    - **Jul 28, 2026:** second-degree chemical (alkali dust) burn to a worker replacing a screw conveyor at the kiln baghouse; 13 restricted days.
  - Recurring non-injury events: workers trapped for more than 30 minutes in plant elevators (tower, coal, homogenizing silo), about 10 times since 2020.
  - No fatalities in the data.
- **Trend:**
  - Staffing: rising, not falling, through 2026 Q2 (apart from the quarry and office reductions).
  - Violations: total counts fell after the 2021–22 peak (87 and 109), but **severity rose in 2026**: S&S share 39%, 7 high-negligence findings and the 104(d)(1). The 2026 S&S count (12) already exceeds any full year since 2015.
  - The two 2026 injuries listed in the data involved workers with under a year at the mine.
  - All of this precedes any announced reduction. Whether a smaller crew changes the pattern is not answerable from this record.

## 4. Trends
1. **Headcount is up, not down, through 2026 Q2.** The total went from about 95 (2020) to 113–119 (2025 Q3–2026 Q2). All of the growth is in the plant/mill subunit, from about 80 to about 104–107.
2. **The quarry was already cut to a skeleton crew in 2024 Q2**: 7 people down to 3, at a flat 1,560 hours. Office staff went from 9 to 6 in 2026 Q1.
3. **Hours per employee are steady** at about 500–585 per quarter. There is no overtime signal.
4. **Violation severity rose in 2026.** The count is lower than 2021–22, but the S&S share is highest in the series, there are the most high-negligence findings since 2021, and there is the first 104(d)(1) since 2012. Repeated themes: housekeeping, guarding, access, uncorrected equipment defects.
5. **Injury frequency is low to moderate.** The NFDL rate was 0 in 2023–24 and about 1.6–1.7 in 2025–26.
